## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "In a week of claims and counter-claims, the only safe position is the verified one."

This was a week in which it became harder to know what was true. Iran says it struck seven ships on a single night. The United States says Iran is inventing attacks to push up oil prices before the midterm elections. UKMTO, the agency most of us rely on, says that most attacked ships are no longer reporting to it at all. Somewhere between those three positions is the real picture, and our job is to find it before we commit a ship, a cargo or a crew.

What we can confirm is serious enough. A Marshall Islands VLCC lost power after a strike off Al Jubail, and all 15 of her Indian crew were taken off safely. Another tanker was hit by a grenade fired from a small boat. A Kuwaiti tanker, attacked once already in September, was set on fire off the Omani coast. And in Saudi Arabia, Houthi attacks killed four people at Dammam airport and near the Shaybah field. Our thoughts are with their families.

For those of us in Southeast Asia, one item this week deserves particular attention. On 21 October OFAC designated a Singapore shipping agency for providing port services and bunkering to Iranian-linked tonnage. Sanctions risk is not only about the ship alongside; it is about every agent, supplier and service provider in the chain. We screen all of them.

On fuel, the Fujairah premium over Singapore has narrowed to about US$33–40, the lowest in this series. That is not good news for buyers in our region: it reflects Singapore becoming expensive, not Fujairah becoming cheap. Lead times remain long. Plan early.

Finally, freight. VLCC rates eased again this week, as we expected, but they remain at levels none of us would have believed a year ago. Discipline, not optimism, is still the right approach.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 17–23 October 2026.

**21–23 October · Al Jazeera / Reuters / Gulf News — Houthi attacks deep into Saudi Arabia; Aramco evacuates Dhahran.** An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam on 21 October killed two people and wounded eight; a drone aimed at an Aramco facility at Shaybah was intercepted, and two people were killed. The Houthis also claimed attacks on Abha airport, the defence ministry in Riyadh and Aramco facilities at Yanbu. On 22 October Aramco ordered staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters, and the US State Department ordered diplomats and their families to leave Saudi Arabia.

**16–21 October · UKMTO / Splash247 / gCaptain / SAFETY4SEA — confirmed attacks off Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iraq and Oman.** The Marshall Islands VLCC ST3 was hit about 68 nm north-east of Al Jubail on 16 October; fire in the engine room, loss of power, all 15 Indian crew evacuated. A second tanker nearby was hit by a rocket-propelled grenade from a small boat. A tug was struck off Khor Fakkan on 18 October (one minor injury). Soyia was hit off Al Faw, Iraq, on 20 October with no damage, and the Kuwaiti tanker Al Dasma caught fire off Bukha, Oman, on 21 October; the fire was put out and the crew are safe.

**21–22 October · IRGC / gCaptain / Daily Caller — Iran claims seven ships hit; Washington says the claims are fake.** The IRGC Navy said it struck four ships conducting STS transfers in the northern Gulf and that three more hit mines west of Hormuz. No vessel was named and UKMTO has no matching reports. President Trump said Iran is "making up" attacks to drive up oil before the midterms. The White House said the US will not attack Iran before 3 November; reports say strikes are being planned for after the elections.

**21 October · US Treasury / gCaptain — OFAC targets Iran's shipping network, including a Singapore agency.** Under "Operation Economic Outcast", Treasury designated the law firm Karnakis and Karnakis (Panama and Greece), ship manager Roxette Maritime, Amigoz Singapore PTE Ltd, the Dubai agency Whales Shipping Line and nine vessels. Treasury said Amigoz provided port services for an Iranian crude shipment and bunkered the sanctioned vessel ZIXUAN.

**19–23 October · Reuters / ENGINE / Rigzone / Integr8 — oil at a five-month high; freight above US$20 a barrel.** Brent closed near US$117. An oil spill was reported off Umm Al Quwain, UAE, on 23 October; the source is unknown. Libya shut a pipeline valve from the Hamada and Tahara fields, while Kuwait's exports are back near pre-war levels. Goldman Sachs says average crude freight is above US$20/bbl, about seven times a year ago. The first Russian diesel cargo under the new US deal is on its way to New York.
