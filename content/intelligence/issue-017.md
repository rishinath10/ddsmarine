## This Week at a Glance

The war widened this week. Iran struck at US positions in Bahrain and Kuwait for the first time in two months, the US hit a missile site on Qeshm Island, and the Houthis fired missiles at Riyadh and Jizan. In the strait, tankers were struck almost every day north of Khasab, and one seafarer is missing. ## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "When a seafarer goes missing, the price of oil tells us nothing about the state of the sea."

On 17 November a tanker was struck about 13 nautical miles north of Khasab, and one of her crew is missing. My thoughts are with that seafarer and his family, and with the crews of the other tankers struck this week. Almost every day from Sunday to Wednesday, a ship was hit in the same narrow stretch of water north of the Musandam Peninsula. Most of the hits were to engine rooms.

The conflict also widened beyond the sea. Iran fired at US positions in Bahrain and Kuwait, the US struck back at Qeshm Island, and the Houthis fired missiles at Riyadh. Three US aircraft carriers are now in the region. Gulf governments have called this a dangerous escalation.

And yet oil fell for the third week in a row. Saudi Arabia, Iraq and others are exporting more, the IEA expects a surplus, and the market has decided the war is a shipping problem rather than an oil problem. That may be right for traders. It is not right for the owners, managers and crews who must take ships into those waters.

For us in Southeast Asia, the practical message this week is about time, not price. Singapore VLSFO is cheaper, but it now takes 15 to 17 days to deliver, and LSMGO 12 to 14. Book early, and confirm in writing.

Another development goes to the heart of our own business. On 18 November CENTCOM said that a vessel receiving cargo at sea from a blockaded ship may itself be treated as breaking the blockade, even if it never enters Iranian waters. Iran is gathering tankers off Kharg Island, and one sanctioned tanker has already transferred part of its cargo to another ship. Every STS operator, including DDS, must know exactly whose cargo is being transferred.

One more item matters to our country. Reuters reports that China's imports of crude from Malaysia, long used as a transshipment point for Iranian oil, fell 84% in October from a year earlier. Whatever the reason, it is a reminder that Malaysian waters are watched closely. Our screening must reflect that.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 14–20 November 2026.

**15–18 November · UKMTO / gCaptain / Ship Universe / Windward — tankers struck almost daily north of Khasab; one seafarer missing.** UKMTO reported a tanker attacked eastbound in the Arabian Gulf on 15 November (crew safe), an outbound tanker struck by a projectile with an engine-room fire on 16 November (crew accounted for), and a second tanker disabled by an engine-room fire about 7 nm north of Khasab the same evening. On 17 November a tanker was struck about 13 nm north of Khasab; the fire was put out, but one crew member is missing. On 18 November another tanker's engine room was hit about 6 nm north of Khasab; all crew are safe. ADNOC confirmed one of its vessels was attacked in transit on 17 November. Windward counted five tankers struck between 11 and 18 November.

**13–18 November · Al Jazeera / Washington Post / CNN — Iran strikes at Bahrain and Kuwait; US hits Qeshm.** Iran targeted a US military site in Bahrain on 13 November, its first such strike on a Gulf neighbour in two months, and Bahrain has since faced four Iranian attacks in six days. After an Iranian drone attack on a US radar site in Bahrain on 17 November, the US struck a missile launch site on Qeshm Island. Iran then targeted an airbase in Kuwait used by US forces. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the GCC and the Arab League condemned the attacks.

**19–20 November · oilprice.com / ENGINE — Houthis fire at Riyadh and Jizan.** Media reports said Houthi strikes hit Riyadh's financial district and the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, and that a petrochemicals company in Jubail was hit. ENGINE reported Houthi missile attacks on Riyadh and the Jizan region, home to a 400,000 b/d refinery. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed the details.

**18–20 November · oilprice.com / ENGINE / Trading Economics — IAEA Board: Iran refuses inspectors; Trump defers decision.** IAEA Director General Grossi told the Board the agency has no access to Iran's nuclear facilities. Iran's ambassador said Iran will not admit inspectors while facing US strike threats. President Trump indicated a decision on renewed strikes will wait until after the start of next year and said he was in no rush. Iran and Oman held talks on security in the strait.

**17–20 November · Windward / Bloomberg / Kpler / UANI / CENTCOM — Iran gathers tankers at Kharg; US extends the blockade to STS transfers.** Iran has positioned more than 14 tankers off Kharg Island, many with AIS switched off, and IRGC-affiliated Fars News has proposed a convoy through the Gulf of Oman to Asian buyers; no sailing date has been confirmed. On 17 November the US-sanctioned tanker Olim transferred about 200,000 barrels to the smaller Hao Fan near Kharg (Bloomberg, citing Kpler and UANI). On 18 November CENTCOM said ships receiving cargo at sea from blockaded vessels may be deemed in breach of the blockade, even outside Iranian waters. Windward has tracked no visible crude loadings at Kharg since 17 September.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 20 November 2026**

Malaysian transshipment falls sharply: China's crude imports from Malaysia, which Reuters describes as a key transshipment hub for sanctioned Iranian crude, fell 84% year on year to 180,000 b/d in October (via Integr8).

Blockade extended to STS transfers: on 18 November CENTCOM said that vessels receiving cargo at sea from blockaded ships "may be deemed in violation of the blockade" even if they never enter Iranian waters or ports, and may be stopped, boarded, diverted or seized. The statement followed a transfer of about 200,000 barrels from the sanctioned tanker Olim to Hao Fan near Kharg on 17 November; UANI says Hao Fan broadcast a false position near Oman.

No new OFAC Iran designations found this week.

Direct operational implication for DDS: the CENTCOM statement applies directly to STS work: receiving a cargo that originated from a blockaded Iranian vessel can now expose the receiving ship to enforcement anywhere at sea. Lower Iranian volumes through Malaysia do not mean lower risk. If Iran tries to restart exports, shadow-fleet activity off Malaysia could return quickly, and enforcement attention on Malaysian waters is high. Screen every vessel for sanctions, recent port calls and AIS gaps, and keep the records.

| Control | Minimum Action |
|---|---|
| Port-call history | Check AIS and documented port calls for the last 120 days; flag any Iranian port call. |
| Sanctions status | Screen every vessel, owner, manager, agent and supplier on the day, against the current SDN list. |
| STS cargo origin | New this issue: before any STS, obtain the cargo's loading port, bill of lading and the discharging ship's 120-day AIS and STS history; refuse cargo that may originate from a blockaded vessel. |
| AIS gaps | Treat a vessel that has stopped broadcasting near Port Klang or the EOPL as a red flag. |
| Record preservation | Keep screening evidence, approvals, emails, SOF and operational instructions in the job file. |
