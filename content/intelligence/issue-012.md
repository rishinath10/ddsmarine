## This Week at a Glance

The attacks crossed a new line this week: ships are now being sunk. The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride went down after a projectile strike on 13 October, and the bulk carrier Cape Dao, hit on 23 September, also sank. Iran's campaign has widened from tankers to container ships and from the Strait to the Sohar anchorage and the UAE coast, where two Indian seafarers are missing from the product tanker Petro Dolphin. Houthi missiles struck Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran. Oil responded: Brent closed the week near US$109.5 and WTI above US$100. For our corridor the effect is felt directly in fuel: Singapore is short of fuel oil, HSFO has risen about US$100 in two weeks, and VLSFO lead times now run to 18 days.

- **Ships sunk:** GFS Pride (Singapore flag, container ship) sank on 13–14 Oct; all 21 crew evacuated. Cape Dao sank after the 23 Sep strike that killed one seafarer.
- **Attacks spread:** at least 11 named incidents 10–15 Oct, including container ships, a drone strike at Sohar anchorage and a tanker off the UAE. UANI now counts 110 incidents since the war began.
- **Saudi Arabia under fire:** Houthi missiles hit Aramco's Dhahran headquarters (limited fire, no casualties); Riyadh airport has been attacked 20 times in October.
- **No deal:** Iran called the US response to its reopening plan "insufficient"; Washington says the Strait is open. The US disabled the blockade runner Ocean Molica.
- **Supply measures:** the IEA agreed to release the remaining 100m barrels from its March programme; the US is considering easing sanctions on some shadow-fleet tankers to bring freight down.

The bulk carrier Cape Dao, struck on 23 September with the loss of one seafarer, also sank. At Sohar anchorage, two Indian seafarers are still missing from the small product tanker Petro Dolphin. Reuters reported this week that supertanker rates came off their record highs, as higher freight cut some export demand and Washington talked about freeing up sanctioned tankers. Finally, our data. This week we had four sources for Singapore VLSFO, and three of them agreed within US$3. That is the standard we want every week.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 10–16 October 2026.

**13–15 October · UKMTO / Seatrade / MPA Singapore / Euronews — two merchant ships sunk; container ships now targeted.** UKMTO reported four attacks in Hormuz on 13 October, hitting the container ships GFS Pride, GFS Giselle and Al Saadiyat and the tanker Alfonso. GFS Pride caught fire and sank; Singapore's MPA confirmed all 21 crew were evacuated. GFS Giselle reached anchorage with hull damage. The IRGC claimed a ship hit a mine; maritime sources cited a projectile. Cape Dao, struck on 23 September, also sank. Two more tankers were reported on fire near the Strait on 14–15 October.

**10–11 October · Seatrade / Maritime Executive / The National — attacks reach Sohar anchorage and the UAE coast.** The Suezmax Valthera (10 Oct) and the VLCC Angola Prosperity (11 Oct) were hit leaving the Strait; crews safe. A drone struck the small product tanker Petro Dolphin at Sohar anchorage, Oman, on 11 October; nine or ten crew were rescued and two Indian seafarers are missing. The crude carrier Gem No 2 was hit by a drone about 25 km off the UAE; all 22 crew safe.

**13–15 October · The National / Bloomberg / Middle East Eye — Houthis strike Aramco headquarters; Riyadh airport attacked 20 times.** A Houthi missile hit an administrative building at Aramco's Dhahran complex on 13 October, causing a limited fire and no casualties. The Saudi-led coalition said Riyadh's airport had been attacked 20 times since 1 October, and the Houthis have promised further attacks.

**11–14 October · Haaretz / The National / Seatrade — no deal: Iran rejects US response; US disables blockade runner.** Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei called the US response to Iran's seven-condition reopening plan "insufficient". US Secretary of State Rubio said the Strait is open and Iran has lost control of it. US forces disabled the Panama-flagged bulk carrier Ocean Molica for trying to run the blockade; 135 ships have now been turned back.

**14–16 October · oilprice.com / Bloomberg / EIA / Integr8 — supply measures, and a sharp US stock draw.** The IEA agreed to release the outstanding 100m barrels from its March emergency programme. The US is considering easing sanctions on some shadow-fleet tankers to lower freight rates. US crude stocks fell 8.6m barrels in the week to 9 October, the largest draw since the war began, after Hurricane Isaias shut-ins. Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian refineries after a short-lived energy ceasefire.

Three things pushed oil up this week. Ship sinkings and wider attacks raised the risk premium; Kpler data show Hormuz oil flows of about 11.62m bpd in the week to 13 October, against a pre-war baseline of 17.15m. US crude stocks fell 8.6m barrels after Hurricane Isaias, the largest draw since the war began, which lifted WTI above US$100. Hormuz, Gulf anchorages and Saudi Arabia.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 16 October 2026**

Possible easing for shadow-fleet tankers: the US administration is considering easing sanctions on some shadow-fleet tankers to bring freight down; shippers would have to apply to Treasury (Reuters, via oilprice.com). Nothing has been issued yet. EU sanctions would still apply, which ING says could complicate insurance.

Russian diesel deal: the US deal allows Russia to export up to 4.8m tonnes of diesel. House Republicans are moving to block it, and Bulgaria has broken with EU criticism of it.

US blockade: 135 ships turned back, four disabled and two boarded since 14 July (UANI, CENTCOM). The US has destroyed 10 IRGC-linked tankers. About nine small Iranian LPG and naphtha tankers have passed the blockade; no crude-laden tanker has.

Direct operational implication for DDS: UANI reports that the Iran-flagged, OFAC-sanctioned container ships VIRA 1 and FLORA have been at Port Klang's berths 10 and 22, and names the port operator and a ship agent as potentially exposed to secondary sanctions. A falsely flagged vessel was reported heading to Port Klang from Mumbai, due 17 October. About 18 shadow-fleet tankers remain at the eastern OPL. Any service provider in Malaysian ports should screen every counterparty.

| Control | Minimum Action |
|---|---|
| Sanctioned vessels in port | Screen every vessel calling at or alongside your operation against the OFAC SDN list, not only STS counterparties. |
| Shadow-fleet relief | Do not act on reported easing until Treasury publishes the terms; EU sanctions still apply. |
| Russian diesel | Read the OFAC general licence terms and expiry before accepting any Russian-origin product. |
| False flags | Verify flag registration directly with the registry, not only from AIS or documents. |
| Record preservation | Keep screening evidence, approvals, emails, SOF and operational instructions in the job file. |
