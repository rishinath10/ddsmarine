## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "Believe the tracker, not the transcript. That gap is the whole story this week."

Issue 005 recorded the first tentative steps toward de-escalation. That window has closed. On 30 August the US struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island, and within hours Iran had fired ballistic missiles at US-linked bases in Jordan and the UAE. By the next day a supertanker had been holed by naval mines inside the corridor the US Navy calls safe, and a second vessel had been hit by projectiles. A third tanker was struck off Khasab days later. This is not a de-escalation story. It is the sharpest exchange we have recorded since we began this publication.

What I want every reader to sit with is not the violence alone but the arithmetic around it. US officials this week described escorting roughly 40 commercial vessels carrying 18 million barrels through the Strait in a single day — a "wartime high." Kpler's independently tracked count for the same window shows five commodity vessels transiting on Monday, none of them liquid tankers, against a 10-day average of about 14. Both figures cannot be describing the same strait. For operators making real decisions about real tonnage, the practical lesson is to plan against the tracked number, not the announced one, until the two converge.

The second development I want to flag is closer to our own operating discipline. Iran's newly formed Persian Gulf Strait Authority has blacklisted 45 tankers — not fringe operators, but vessels linked to ADNOC Logistics & Services, its Navig8 Tankers subsidiary, and Saudi Arabia's Bahri. Tehran has now said any vessel conducting an STS transfer with a blacklisted ship can itself be added to the list. That is a direct, structural threat to exactly the kind of ship-to-ship operations this company runs every week. It means our counterparty screening cannot stop at the vessel in front of us; it has to extend to that vessel's own recent transit and STS history.

My instruction to our teams: treat every Hormuz-linked nomination as an owner-level decision, screen STS counterparties against the current Iranian blacklist before every job, and build voyage economics around Kpler-tracked flows rather than official claims. This is now a live shooting conflict with a widening credibility gap at its centre, and the operators who plan around the honest number will be the ones who aren't caught out by the next headline.

**The Larak Island exchange.** US Central Command said its forces "took limited, precise action against IRGC mine-laying forces posing an imminent threat in the Strait of Hormuz," striking two rocket launchers on Larak Island on 30 August after observing preparations to fire rockets carrying sea mines into the waterway. Iran's IRGC confirmed casualties among its personnel and vowed retaliation, which arrived within a day: ballistic missiles fired at bases housing US forces in Jordan (Jordan says it intercepted eight incoming missiles) and at an airbase in the UAE. It is the first major exchange of fire between the US and Iran in more than a month.

**Tankers caught in the renewed exchange.** The supertanker Senegal Prosperity was struck by two naval mines while transiting the Strait on 31 August, sparking a fire and forcing it to stop — inside the southern corridor, close to Omani shores, that the US Navy promotes as the strait's safe passage. The Sidr, another Bahri-operated vessel, was hit by projectiles the same day. UKMTO has since logged a further projectile attack on a tanker approximately 17nm east of Khasab, with an earlier vessel hit near Khasab on 29 August. CENTCOM has separately dismissed an IRGC claim of forcing a tanker to turn around without permission as "disinformation" — a reminder that claims from both sides in this conflict require independent verification wherever possible.

**Believe the tracker, not the transcript.** The most consequential number this week may not be a price or a casualty count, but a traffic count. US officials, including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Energy Secretary Chris Wright, have pointed to volumes as high as 17–18 million barrels moving through Hormuz on single days this week, with CNN reporting the US military escorted roughly 40 commercial vessels on Tuesday alone — a "wartime high." Kpler's independently tracked data for the same window shows just five commodity vessels transiting on Monday (four in, one out), none of them liquid tankers, against a trailing 10-day average of about 14. Some of the gap is explained by covert ship-to-ship transfers reported in the Gulf of Oman — CNN observed more than a dozen STS transfers over two days, cargo moving from southern-corridor tankers onto customer vessels waiting outside the Gulf — but the scale of the discrepancy is itself a risk signal.

>
> Active kinetic exchanges have resumed between the US and Iran; multiple tankers have been struck within the reporting window; and a material, unresolved gap exists between official and independently tracked Hormuz traffic. DDS recommends treating every Hormuz-linked nomination as an owner-level decision, obtaining current-week war-risk confirmation before fixing, and planning voyage economics against Kpler-tracked flows rather than official volume claims.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 4 September 2026**

Iran blacklists 45 tankers, extends threat to STS counterparties: Iran's newly established Persian Gulf Strait Authority has blacklisted 45 tankers accused of violating its Strait of Hormuz transit rules, warning that listed vessels face fines, detention and cargo confiscation. The list spans VLCCs, LNG and LPG carriers and clean-product tankers, and includes vessels linked to the United Arab Emirates' ADNOC Logistics & Services and its Navig8 Tankers subsidiary, and Saudi Arabia's national carrier Bahri — not fringe operators, but core Gulf players. Iran has said any vessel conducting an STS transfer with a listed vessel can itself be added to the blacklist, directly extending enforcement risk to counterparties.

War-risk insurance: market indications this week put Gulf war-risk premiums in the region of 3–6% of vessel value for transits, with some Hormuz-specific voyages quoted materially higher. Cargo and STS cover is reported to be becoming more complex to place, consistent with insurers repricing both kinetic risk and the blacklist's counterparty-contagion effect.

Direct operational implication for DDS: the blacklist's STS-contagion clause means vessel screening can no longer stop at the nominated ship. Before accepting any STS counterparty, confirm that vessel's own recent Hormuz transit and STS history is clear of the current 45-vessel list, in addition to standard OFAC/SDN screening.
