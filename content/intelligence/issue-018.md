## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 20–27 November 2026.

**20–22 November · UKMTO / JMIC / gCaptain / Maritime Executive — seven ships hit around Musandam in three days.** An outbound tanker was struck near Khasab on 20 November, with a fire on board. On 21 November one tanker was hit on its stern about 11 nm north of Khasab and another in its engine room 17 nm east of Khasab; gCaptain names Torkel and Arina as attacked that day. On the evening of 22 November three eastbound VLCCs were hit between 20:25 and 20:42 UTC, and a bulk carrier, CTI Helen, was hit and set on fire at the Dibba anchorage off Khor Fakkan. No casualties were reported. JMIC counted 10 tankers struck in the strait in one week and rates the threat "severe".

**25–26 November · Saudi Energy Ministry / The National / Argus — Houthis strike at Saudi refineries; a worker is killed at Jazan.** Saudi air defences intercepted four drones launched at the 550,000 b/d Ras Tanura refinery on 25 November; the Houthis claimed a hit. On 26 November a "hostile aerial projectile" struck the 400,000 b/d Jazan refinery on the Red Sea coast. A fire was brought under control, some operations were halted temporarily, and one Nepali worker was killed. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed the launch platform in Yemen's Hodeidah governorate. The Houthis also claimed strikes on the ports of Jeddah and Jazan.

**26 November · Kuwait Defence Ministry / Al Jazeera / ABC — two Italian technicians killed in Kuwait.** Kuwait said Iranian drones targeted a "vital site", which Italy described as an air-defence surveillance station. Debris from intercepted drones killed two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia and wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani. Iran's military "categorically" denied involvement. The GCC Secretary-General condemned the attack as "brutal".

**20–24 November · oilprice.com / The National / Iran International — convoy talk fades; Trump points to the New Year.** IRGC-linked media spent four days promoting a tanker convoy from Kharg to break the US blockade. By 24 November state media had walked the claims back, and reports said Iran's Supreme National Security Council had halted the dispatch of 14 tankers; this has not been verified. President Trump said "they'll all be at the bottom of the sea" if a convoy tried, and that major strikes could resume after the New Year if no deal is reached.

**24–27 November · ENGINE / Integr8 / The National — oil rises for the first time in four weeks.** Brent fell to about US$88.5 early in the week, then rose for three sessions on the attacks in Saudi Arabia and the strait, reaching about US$97 on Friday. Kpler data show oil leaving Hormuz averaged 9.25m b/d in the seven days to 24 November, 54% of pre-war levels. OPEC+ meets on Sunday and is expected to keep its targets unchanged.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 27 November 2026**

OFAC, 20 November: four Iranian individuals designated as "regime insiders" profiting from the oil sector. OFAC, 23 November: action against Iran's shadow banking networks, naming 22 entities (16 in Dubai) and eight individuals. Neither action lists vessels or entities in Malaysia or Singapore.

Malaysia under scrutiny: on 16 November the Washington Free Beacon, citing UANI, alleged a network of more than 200 vessels operating about 70 km off Malaysia, supported by Malaysian supply boats. A US official was quoted as saying "Malaysia will have to choose." The Malaysian government did not respond. These are allegations, not findings.

Blockade: one report (Seatrade, via Wisevoter) says the OFAC-sanctioned chemical tanker Voyager Haven crossed the US blockade line at the mouth of the Gulf of Oman on 26 November. We have not been able to confirm this with a second source. The US is also investigating possible Iranian involvement in cyber attacks on as many as 50 tankers (ENGINE).

Direct operational implication for DDS: the 23 November action targets the money behind Iran's trade, largely through Dubai trading houses. Payment chains matter as much as ships. Screen the counterparties that pay and are paid, not only the vessels. With US attention on Malaysian waters, assume every STS operation off Malaysia may be examined, and keep the evidence that it was screened.
