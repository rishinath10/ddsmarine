## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 12–18 September 2026.

**17 September · Reuters / oilprice.com — Saudi East-West pipeline damage worse than first thought.** Reuters reported three pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline were damaged in the prior week's drone attack, citing satellite imagery and industry sources; earlier assessments had identified only two. Repairs could take five to six weeks, though partial pumping may resume sooner. The system normally carries 4–5 million bpd of the route's 7 million bpd capacity. Aramco is targeting a partial restart within days per Bloomberg, and Yanbu inventories have fallen from ~21m barrels in July to below 15m barrels, per Kpler — at 3.5m bpd throughput, little more than four days of theoretical cover.

**16 September · oilprice.com / Bloomberg — tanker rates break US$1 million a day for the first time.** Bloomberg reported Gulf-pickup tanker rates as high as US$1.035 million/day, citing Baltic Exchange data, with Gulf of Oman-to-China business (outside Hormuz) quoted around US$644,000/day. Second-hand tanker values have moved sharply: a second-hand VLCC now reportedly commands roughly US$182m against ~US$130m for a newbuild, according to Argus; second-hand Suezmaxes and Aframaxes are similarly commanding premiums over new tonnage.

**September · Wall Street Journal — US investigates suspected cyberattack on two tankers near Gibraltar.** US federal authorities are investigating a suspected cyberattack on two tankers — one carrying crude, one LNG — travelling from Europe toward the US Gulf Coast, after both were flagged for inspection on arrival in August. The incidents are believed to have originated near Gibraltar, a strait carrying roughly 300 vessels a day. US Coast Guard officials noted the safety risk of any system fault aboard a laden tanker.

**18 September · Times of Israel / Iranian state media — IRGC Navy strikes tanker attempting "illegal passage".** Iran's IRGC Navy said it struck a Togo-flagged oil tanker attempting what it called an unauthorised passage through the Strait of Hormuz; the vessel caught fire and came to a halt. **September · oilprice.com — Congress sends Russia-Iran sanctions bill to Trump; China presses Iran on the Houthis.** The US House passed legislation authorising tariffs of up to 100% on countries including China and India that continue buying Russian energy. Separately, oilprice.com reports China has pressed Iran to rein in Houthi activity as Gulf tensions weigh on regional trade. The IEA reported global observed oil inventories fell 95 million barrels in August — a cumulative 507 million barrel drawdown since February, or roughly 2.8 million bpd.

## Data Integrity & Methodology

Why our numbers move, and what we are changing from this issue on.

Our own team flagged, correctly, that bunker and freight figures in recent issues have looked inconsistent from one day to the next. They have — because the underlying market moves within a wide daily range, and because different licensed sources publish different snapshots of it. This section sets out how we now handle that, rather than leaving readers to guess.

**Four rules, applied from this issue:**

| Rule | What It Means Here |
|---|---|
| 1. Stated cut-off | Every issue now states the exact data cut-off on its dashboard. This issue: Friday 18 September 2026. A figure is only ever valid as of that moment. |
| 2. Ranges, not points | Where our source itself publishes a daily High/Low, we report that range plus the index close — e.g. Singapore VLSFO US$900–928/mt (index 913.50) — instead of a single number that implies more precision than the market actually offers. |
| 3. Dual-source cross-check | Where we hold more than one subscription or source for the same benchmark, we show both and disclose the variance rather than picking one silently. See Global Oil Market, below. |
| 4. Contract month stated | Crude and product quotes will state the delivery/contract month where it materially affects comparability — front-month WTI and a forward month are not the same number. |

*Worked example this issue: Ship & Bunker's Friday index puts Singapore VLSFO at 913.50, inside a US$900–927.50 daily range. Our Integr8 subscription's Thursday indication was 935. Neither figure is wrong — one is a same-day index close, the other a broker's early indication from the previous session. Reporting both, with dates, is more useful to a reader making a bunkering decision than silently averaging them or picking whichever number looks cleaner.*

**What this does not change:** DDS does not reproduce Baltic Exchange's proprietary index values, and freight sentiment continues to be expressed as an independent 1–5 DDS score rather than a licensed benchmark (see DDS Freight Market Monitor). Where a Baltic Exchange-derived figure is reported by a named wire service — for example, Bloomberg's reporting on Gulf tanker rates — DDS may cite that as news, attributed to the wire service, in the same way any other syndicated fact is cited.

## Geopolitical & Route Risk

Two chokepoints, one drone war — Hormuz and the Saudi East-West pipeline.

**The bypass becomes a casualty.** Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline exists specifically so the kingdom does not have to send all of its crude through Hormuz. This week that logic broke down. Reuters reported three pumping stations damaged, worse than the two-station assessment made after the initial attack, with repairs estimated at five to six weeks. Yanbu's buffer stocks have fallen from roughly 21 million barrels in July to under 15 million, per Kpler — at current throughput, barely four days of theoretical cover. Aramco is racing to restore around half of capacity within days by bypassing the damaged sections, but full service is not expected soon.

The market's response has been to route around the routing-around: Saudi Arabia is reportedly selling as much as 20 million barrels on the spot market this week, with delivery via ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait — avoiding sending buyers' own tankers deep into the Gulf, but adding a further layer of STS activity and cost precisely where our own operational focus sits. Japanese refiners have separately rushed to secure Oman crude as an alternative to the shut pipeline.

**A widening list, not a shrinking one.** Windward's tracking shows Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority has issued blacklist tranches on 24 August, 2 September and 14 September, taking the list to 78 vessels — up from the 45 reported in Issue 006. Windward's analysis found the listed vessels had all used either the unauthorised southern corridor through Omani waters or Iran's own northern corridor without paying tolls; inclusion on the list does not appear to have stopped any of them continuing to trade in the region.
