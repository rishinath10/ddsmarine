## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "Freight has halved. The attacks have not. We must not mistake a cheaper market for a safer sea."

This week UKMTO recorded at least seven tankers attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz. The crude tanker Dominica Prosperity was hit in the engine room and set on fire off Ras Al Khaimah; the UAE Coast Guard took off her 22 crew, 19 of them Indian. Crew members of Fujairah Energy were injured and taken ashore for treatment. I wish them a full recovery, and I thank the coast guards and crews who keep answering these calls.

At the same time, the markets moved the other way. Brent ended the week lower. VLCC rates are about half of October's record, and Suezmax and Aframax rates have fallen sharply. The reason is supply: Saudi Arabia, Iraq and the wider OPEC+ group have restored most of their output using routes around Hormuz, and more ships are free to carry it.

For the first time since we started the DDS Freight Sentiment Index, we have lowered our crude tanker scores, from 5 to 4. That reflects the market, not the danger. The danger is if owners, charterers and crews start to treat the war as normal. Seven attacks in a week is not normal.

In our own region, Singapore VLSFO has climbed back to about US$900/mt and lead times remain 10 to 17 days. Fuel-oil stocks rose 4% this month, which should help over time. Plan ahead and confirm stems in writing.

Finally, a note on diplomacy. The US says it does not need a deal; Iran says there are no talks. Our planning assumption is that nothing changes at sea in the coming weeks.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 7–13 November 2026.

**10–13 November · UKMTO / JMIC / gCaptain / Middle East Eye — at least seven tankers attacked in a week.** The Liberia-flagged crude tanker Dominica Prosperity was hit in the engine room about 14 nm north-west of Ras Al Khaimah on 11 November; the fire was still being fought when UKMTO issued its warning, and the UAE Coast Guard evacuated all 22 crew. Crew members of Fujairah Energy were injured on 10 November and taken ashore. NCC Fajr was hit north of Khasab (crew safe), an ADNOC vessel was damaged in UAE waters, and a tanker was struck 19 nm north-east of Lima, Oman. UKMTO's weekly overview counted at least seven tankers attacked in the week to 11 November.

**7 November · CENTCOM / Maritime Executive — US disables bulker leaving an Iranian port.** US forces fired on and disabled the St Kitts and Nevis-flagged bulk carrier Huda (24,993 dwt, Turkish-managed) about 18 nm east of Khor Fakkan after she visited an Iranian port. CENTCOM said it told the crew to leave the targeted spaces first; UKMTO said their status was unknown. CENTCOM says 147 vessels have been redirected since the blockade restarted in July.

**8–13 November · CBS News / Trading Economics / oilprice.com / ENGINE — talks stall: "We don't need a deal".** Early in the week President Trump said the war could end soon and that new strikes were "highly unlikely" while talks continued. By Thursday he said "we don't need a deal with Iran." Iran denied any talks, said enriched nuclear material would not leave the country, and on 13 November ruled out the return of UN inspectors. Iran's negotiator Ghalibaf said there are "no free waters" in the strait.

**8–9 November · Al Jazeera / Saudi Ministry of Energy — East–West pipeline targeted; Aramco building in Riyadh damaged.** Saudi Arabia said its East–West pipeline was targeted in the Medina region on 9 November; a limited fire was put out, with no injuries and no effect on operations. An Aramco building in Riyadh was damaged in what was described as an attempted attack, after Houthi claims of strikes on Riyadh.

**11–13 November · Integr8 / oilprice.com / Reuters — Gulf supply returns; freight falls.** OPEC+ output jumped 2.04m b/d in October to 41.23m b/d, 97% of February levels, led by Saudi Arabia; Iraq's output is the highest since 2019. VLCC rates are down about 50% from October's record, and Asian buyers are turning to African and Brazilian crude. ASEAN agreed a joint crude stockpiling scheme with storage in Indonesia and Thailand.

**The first downgrade.** Reuters reports VLCC rates down about 50% from October's all-time highs, with Suezmax and Aframax rates also falling sharply. Vortexa says laden VLCCs and Suezmaxes in East Asia have risen about 15% since early September, and Energy Aspects describes long-haul VLCC rates as softer. ## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 13 November 2026**

Blockade enforcement: US forces disabled the bulk carrier Huda on 7 November as she left after calling at an Iranian port. Maritime Executive reports she does not appear to be under sanctions; enforcement applied because of the port call. CENTCOM says 147 commercial vessels have been redirected since the blockade restarted in mid-July, five disabled in the current effort and ten tankers destroyed.

No new OFAC Iran designations found this week. The 21 October action, which included Amigoz Singapore PTE Ltd, remains the most recent we have seen.

Iran's finances: oilprice.com reports Iran is preparing a budget with little oil revenue as the blockade continues.

Direct operational implication for DDS: the Huda case shows that a vessel need not be sanctioned to be targeted: a call at an Iranian port is enough. Check the recent port-call history of every vessel we work with, not only its sanctions status. A vessel that has called at an Iranian port since July should be treated as high risk.

## Sources & Disclaimer

DDS Weekly Maritime Intelligence combines public information, official releases, wire reporting carried by named outlets, and operator-focused analysis. Every price is shown with a stated cut-off and, where the source publishes one, a range. Where we hold more than one source for a benchmark, all are shown with the variance disclosed; where only one source is available, we say so. A price page is not used unless its figures can be shown to be current. Estimates derived by DDS are labelled. Claims made by parties to the conflict are labelled until independently verified. DDS freight commentary is independent market analysis; proprietary third-party assessments are not reproduced unless authorised. DDS market sentiment indicators represent DDS Marine's own analytical assessment and are not third-party benchmarks.
