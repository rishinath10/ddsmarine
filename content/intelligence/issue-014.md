## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "When Singapore fuel costs more than Fujairah fuel, the war has reached our own bunker market."

This week a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was disabled by explosives attached to her hull from a small boat. It appears to be the first time in this war that such an attack has succeeded, and it changes the threat that crews face. Drones and missiles can be tracked; a small boat alongside at night is much harder to see. I am thankful that all 27 Indian seafarers aboard Louise 1 are safe, as are the 25 crew taken off a burning ship near Musandam by the Royal Navy of Oman.

The claims continue alongside the facts. Iran says five more tankers were struck on routes it does not recognise; CENTCOM says the main lanes are clear of mines. As I wrote last week, we act on what is verified, and we plan for what is claimed.

For our region, the most important number this week is in fuel. Singapore VLSFO has passed US$1,000 a tonne and is now slightly more expensive than in Fujairah. In September a buyer saved more than US$100 a tonne by taking fuel in Singapore. That advantage has gone. Lead times of about two weeks, and longer from some suppliers, mean that planning is now more important than price.

On freight, rates continued to ease as more ships became available, including tankers that until recently traded in the shadow fleet. That is good for charterers, but our counterparties must still be screened ship by ship.

Finally, the calendar. The US midterm elections are on Tuesday. Reports that Washington has told its Gulf allies to prepare for renewed strikes after the vote should be taken seriously. Owners with ships near the Gulf in the first half of November should review their plans now.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 24–30 October 2026.

**29–30 October · UKMTO / Euronews / Financial Times / Indian government — limpet mines disable a tanker in Hormuz; second ship ablaze off Musandam.** A small boat attached two explosive devices to the Panama-flagged tanker Louise 1 about 25 nm north-east of Khasab on 29 October. The blasts caused an engine-room fire and temporarily disabled the ship; the fire was put out, all 27 Indian crew are safe, and she resumed her voyage. The Liberia-flagged tanker Arzanah was hit by a projectile the same day; her two Indian crew are safe. On 30 October a Panama-flagged vessel caught fire about 1.6 nm off Musandam and the Royal Navy of Oman evacuated 25 crew.

**29–30 October · IRGC / SAFETY4SEA / CENTCOM — Iran claims five more tankers hit; US says lanes are clear of mines.** The IRGC said three tankers struck mines on the southern route on 29 October, and that two more had "incidents" on unauthorised routes on 30 October; none was named. UKMTO relayed one sighting of a vessel on fire about 9 nm north of Khasab. CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said the main shipping lanes have been cleared of Iranian mines.

**27–30 October · oilprice.com / CBS News / Al Jazeera — fire at Ras Tanura; Houthis claim strikes on Saudi refineries.** A fire at the 550,000 b/d Ras Tanura refinery on 27 October was quickly contained, Saudi authorities said. The Houthis then claimed strikes on Ras Tanura, Rabigh, Abqaiq, the East–West pipeline and two airports; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed them. Saudi-led forces said they shot down two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. Exxon and Chevron executives cancelled a Gulf visit on security grounds.

**25–30 October · Reuters / WSJ / Axios / CBS News — talks and deadlines: Vance letter, Qatari draft, post-election strike planning.** Qatar gave Iran a new draft deal on 25 October; an Iranian official called it "unacceptable", and a reply is expected this weekend. Vice President Vance wrote to Mohsen Rezaei about restarting talks. At the same time, US officials reportedly told Gulf allies to prepare for a brief but intense campaign after the 3 November midterms; President Trump has not made a final decision.

**28–30 October · oilprice.com / Bloomberg / Vortexa — Europe releases oil; Qatar extends LNG force majeure; freight eases.** European governments will release 42m barrels over three months, half of it diesel, as part of the IEA-coordinated programme. QatarEnergy extended its LNG force majeure to the end of 2026, and European gas reached its highest since 2022. Vortexa data show crude freight on long-haul routes to Asia about US$10/bbl below early-October peaks.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 30 October 2026**

Former shadow-fleet tankers return: Bloomberg reports that VLCCs which previously carried Venezuelan crude are now lifting crude from the Gulf after being delisted or licensed. Energy Secretary Chris Wright says "there is no removing of sanctions" and that the ships will no longer carry sanctioned oil. The terms are not public in full.

US blockade: JMIC said on 28 October that the US is blockading all Iranian ports and coastlines.

No new OFAC Iran designations found this week. The 21 October action, which included the Singapore agency Amigoz Singapore PTE Ltd, remains the most recent; Treasury Secretary Bessent warned: "we will find you and sanction you."

Russian diesel: the first US-licensed Russian diesel cargo faces legal questions at its New York destination, according to oilprice.com.

Direct operational implication for DDS: a former shadow-fleet tanker may now be trading lawfully under a licence or after delisting, or it may not. Do not rely on a vessel's past or on press reports: check the current OFAC SDN list, ask for the licence where one is claimed, and keep the evidence. UANI's latest available count (22 October) showed about 24 tankers on its watch list at Malaysia's eastern OPL; its site could not be reached at this week's cut-off.
