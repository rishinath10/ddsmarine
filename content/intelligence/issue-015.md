## CEO Market Commentary

> **Capt. Dinesh Naidu KC — Founder & Chairman, DDS Marine Group**
>
> "Oil traders have priced in peace. The ships in the Gulf are still waiting for it."

This was the week the market decided the war might be ending. After the US elections, Brent fell by more than US$15 a barrel at its lowest point, the steepest weekly fall since March 2020. In Singapore, a tonne of VLSFO that cost more than US$1,000 last Friday cost US$820–885 this Friday. HSFO fell by about US$200. For every owner and operator in our corridor, that is real money saved.

But I would ask our readers to look at what happened at sea in the same week. A tanker was struck by two projectiles off Saudi Arabia on Sunday night. Another was hit north of Qatar on Wednesday night, and we still do not know the condition of her crew. Iran says it has destroyed four tankers with mines. JMIC says further deliberate attacks are "highly likely". About 490 ships were waiting outside the strait at the start of the week. None of that is consistent with a war that is over.

India's HPCL said this week that owners are pulling out of Hormuz voyages at the last minute. I understand why. The decision to send a ship and her crew into the Gulf should rest on the threat, not on the oil price.

So my advice is simple. Use the lower prices: this is a good moment to secure bunkers and to review fuel budgets for the weeks ahead. Do not use them as a reason to relax security, insurance or screening. If a deal comes, we will report it. Until then, we plan for the war that is still happening.

A note on our data: our two price sources for Singapore differ by about US$60 this week, a sign of how fast the market moved. We show both.

## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 31 October–6 November 2026.

**2–6 November · oilprice.com / Trading Economics / ENGINE / ING — the midterm sell-off: Brent's steepest weekly fall since March 2020.** Brent closed Monday at US$104.77, down 7.6%. On Tuesday it fell more than US$6 in minutes on unconfirmed reports of a US–Iran breakthrough, then recovered. After results that ING called a "Democrats' clean sweep", it touched about US$94.66 on Wednesday, the lowest since 4 September. President Trump said gasoline prices explained his party's losses, and Vice President Vance said the war was the reason. Brent recovered to about US$98 by Friday as attacks continued.

**1–5 November · UKMTO / gCaptain / Baird Maritime / Reuters — tankers hit off Saudi Arabia and Qatar; Iran claims mine strikes.** A tanker was struck on the port side by two projectiles about 72 nm east of Ras Al Khafji, Saudi Arabia, late on 1 November; all crew are accounted for. Another tanker was hit by two projectiles about 50 nm north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, late on 4 November; her crew's condition was not known. The IRGC claimed four tankers destroyed by mines south of the strait and two vessels seized (4 November), and two tankers and a tug on fire (5 November). Reuters counted at least five incidents since Sunday.

**2–4 November · The National / WSJ / Financial Times — limited US strike on Qeshm; Trump says no immediate plans for more.** After an Iranian missile shot down a US MQ-9 drone east of the strait, a US fighter destroyed the launcher on Qeshm Island on the night of 2 November, which US officials called "self-defence". The Wall Street Journal described it as one of a series of small, limited strikes. Asked whether he would strike Iran after the elections, President Trump said "no", and he told the Financial Times there were no immediate plans.

**3–6 November · Trading Economics / Qatar MFA / oilprice.com — proposals through Qatar; Iran sets its conditions.** Qatar says both sides are reviewing proposals. Iran has reportedly sent a nuclear proposal to Washington through Qatar, but says the US blockade must be lifted, sanctions eased and frozen assets released before it fully reopens Hormuz. A senior Iranian official dismissed reports of a breakthrough. The Houthis claimed an attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport; Saudi authorities did not confirm it.

**5–6 November · Integr8 / Reuters / oilprice.com — signs of more crude: Aramco cuts Asian prices, Dubai flips to contango.** Saudi Aramco cut its December Arab Light price for Asia by US$2.00/bbl to the lowest since May 2020. The Dubai market moved into contango for the first time since early July, a sign of ample Middle East supply. UAE fuel-oil exports to Singapore and Malaysia reached about 729,000 tonnes in October, an eight-month high. Bahrain's Sitra refinery is expected back at full capacity by the end of November.

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 6 November 2026**

Sanctions are now part of the negotiation: Iran says the easing of sanctions and the release of frozen assets must come before it fully reopens Hormuz. Any deal is likely to change the rules on Iranian oil and shipping, but nothing has changed yet. Until a change is published by OFAC, all existing designations stand.

No new OFAC Iran designations were found this week. The 21 October action, which included Amigoz Singapore PTE Ltd, remains the most recent we have seen.

US blockade: the US blockade of Iranian ports remains in place. The IRGC claims it seized two vessels on 4 November; this has not been confirmed.

Former shadow-fleet tankers: as reported last week, some previously sanctioned tankers are trading Gulf crude after being delisted or licensed. Check each one.

Direct operational implication for DDS: talk of a deal creates a specific risk. Counterparties may claim that sanctions "no longer apply" before any change is published. Until OFAC issues a general licence or removes a designation, treat every listed vessel, company and person as sanctioned. UANI's latest count we could read (22 October) showed about 24 tankers on its watch list at Malaysia's eastern OPL.
