## This Week at a Glance

Iran has turned the recovery in Gulf exports into a target. At least 13 tankers were hit in and around Hormuz between 29 September and 6 October, the most in any week of the war, and on 7 October a tanker was struck 51 nautical miles off Qatar, the first attack that deep in the Gulf in nearly a month. Visible Hormuz transits fell to their lowest since July, yet Middle East crude exports held near pre-war levels because Saudi Arabia and others moved more oil out through the Red Sea and the Gulf of Oman coast. VLCC earnings set another record near US$1.4 million a day. Brent ended the week about 2% higher, helped by Hurricane Isaias shutting in US Gulf output. For our corridor: the attacks are now reaching LPG and LNG carriers and ships inside the Gulf, and Singapore fuel stocks are tight, with lead times of 10–15 days.

## CEO Market Commentary

For two weeks we have written about Gulf exports recovering under fire. This week the fire increased. At least 13 tankers were struck in eight days, more than in any week since the war began, and the attacks spread: to an LNG carrier, to an LPG carrier, and on 7 October to a tanker 51 nautical miles off Qatar, deep inside the Gulf. On the On Peace alone, at least 12 of 19 crew were injured. These are seafarers doing their jobs, and their safety has to come first in every decision we make about routing and nomination.

The market's response tells us something important. Visible traffic through Hormuz fell to its lowest since July, yet total Middle East crude exports held near pre-war levels. Saudi Arabia pushed the East-West pipeline back up to 5.8 million barrels a day, and exports from the Red Sea and the Gulf of Oman coast rose to more than double their pre-war level. Two developments may lower the temperature. President Trump says there will be no US strikes on Iran before the 3 November midterm elections, and Iran is reviewing the US response to its seven-day plan to reopen the Strait. Fresh checks this week show that one of our bunker sources was serving out-of-date prices, and the true premium was much smaller. The details are in our Data Integrity section. ## This Week in Review

The week's principal developments, 3–9 October 2026.

**29 September – 8 October · Reuters / UKMTO / UANI / Seatrade — tanker attacks reach their highest weekly level of the war.** Maritime security sources counted at least 12 attacks on oil, LNG and LPG tankers around Hormuz in the week to 5 October, the most of any week since the war began; UANI counts at least 13 between 29 September and 6 October. UKMTO reported nine attacks in the first seven days of October, half of September's total. Since the war began there have been 97 maritime incidents and 24 seafarer deaths (UANI, citing the IMO). See the Hormuz Attack Tracker below.

**7–8 October · UKMTO / Seatrade / gCaptain / oilprice.com — tanker struck off Qatar, the deepest Gulf attack in nearly a month.** The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged chemical/oil tanker Acers was hit by multiple projectiles about 51 nm north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, at around 19:00 UTC on 7 October. UKMTO reported casualties but has not given numbers, and has not attributed the attack. It is the first attack this deep in the Gulf since 9 September.

**6–8 October · Reuters / Kpler / LSEG — Hormuz transits fall to a two-month low; exports shift to other routes.** Kpler counted more than 20 commodity-vessel transits on 4 and 5 October, then 7 on 6 October, the lowest since 23 July, and 10 on 7 October. Crude through the Strait fell 27% from the previous week to about 10.1m bpd (74% of pre-war), but Red Sea and Gulf of Oman coast exports rose to 6.7m bpd, keeping total Middle East crude exports near pre-war levels.

**6–8 October · oilprice.com / Gulf News / Reuters — Houthis hit Saudi airports; coalition strikes back; pipeline at 5.8m bpd.** Houthi drones and missiles hit airports in Riyadh, Abha and Jazan; Saudi authorities reported three people killed and 36 injured. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed 82 Houthi targets. Saudi Arabia's energy minister said East-West pipeline flows had recovered to 5.8m bpd, denying reports of a new shutdown.

**8–9 October · US News / AGBI / Trading Economics / oilprice.com — no US strikes before the midterms; diesel relief measures.** President Trump said the US will not resume strikes on Iran before the 3 November midterms, and Iran said it is reviewing the US response to its seven-day plan to reopen Hormuz. China resumed October fuel exports with a reported 3.7m-tonne quota, and the US issued a temporary licence for Russian diesel supplies (about 22.5m barrels, per Trading Economics). Hurricane Isaias shut in about 63% of US Gulf oil output on 8 October.

Hurricane Isaias then shut in about 63% of US Gulf of Mexico oil output, around 1.28m bpd, and threatened Gulf Coast refineries. Hormuz, the inner Gulf and the Red Sea.

**The Strait is no longer the only front.** Iran's attacks have followed the oil. As exports recovered, the pace of attacks rose; as ships moved to Omani-side corridors with transponders off, attacks continued there; and this week a tanker was hit inside the Gulf off Qatar. The US pause before the midterms lowers the risk of a wider war in the next four weeks but does nothing to stop attacks on merchant ships.

**Hormuz commodity-vessel transits (Kpler):** more than 20 on 4 and 5 October, 7 on 6 October (the lowest since 23 July) and 10 on 7 October, against a pre-war level of about 125 large vessels a day (Reuters).

## Sanctions & Compliance Watch

**Developments — week ending 9 October 2026**

US licence for Russian diesel, 9 October: at the President's direction, OFAC issued a temporary general licence allowing Russian diesel to be supplied to the global market. The volume cited is about 22.5m barrels. Check the exact terms and expiry of the licence before relying on it for any cargo.

Shadow-fleet designations, 8 October: UANI reports that OFAC sanctioned 22 shadow-fleet vessels, ten of them already on UANI's own watch list.

Iran's blacklist is not a guide to risk: none of the four ships attacked on 3–5 October was on the Persian Gulf Strait Authority list (Seatrade). Six Sinokor VLCCs are on the list, and two other Sinokor VLCCs were attacked.

US blockade: as of 5 October, US forces had redirected 130 vessels, disabled three and boarded two (UANI).

Direct operational implication for DDS: UANI reports 17 Iran-flagged cargo ships in or near Malaysia's eastern OPL anchorage on 8 October, together with four Iran-flagged tankers and about 22 shadow-fleet tankers, while at least 24 others have moved north. Three Iran-linked ships remain at Port Klang. Screening in our own waters remains essential.
